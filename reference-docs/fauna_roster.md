# Fauna Roster

This is the enemy roster for TRAWF's threat ecology, the companion to the flora taxonomy. Every enemy is a former functional part of the neurovascular unit gone feral, and the names say so: where the flora are named for the use they were engineered for (Scarpet was a decorative carpet, Climbvine a textile fiber), the fauna are named for the harm they do to you. The two halves of the ecology rhyme, each wearing its old function from opposite ends. This document fixes the names and states each enemy as a designed encounter (its niche, its tell, and what in the party kit answers it), so the set reads as a kit of distinct threats rather than a list of biology notes. Inter-enemy dynamics live in `enemy_ecosystem.md`; visual designs live in the fauna image prompts.

## The names mirror the flora: each enemy wears the harm it does, the way each plant wears its old use

The names vary their formation on purpose, so the roster reads as if named by different hands over time rather than by one committee with one suffix. Four sources are in the mix: an agent noun built from a harm verb (Gnawers, Spikers, Sapscraps), a part standing in for the whole (Ferrules, for the reinforcing iron sleeve its C-shaped body reads as), an effect or condition left behind (Crusts, Flares), and institutional euphemism (Naturalizers, Redactors). The kept biology-Latin names (Hidras, Candids, Meebs, Toxos) are the older institutional holdovers among them. Two rules sit under the variety. The immobile colonizers take condition or terrain readings (Crusts as a mass-noun, with Candids reading as terrain you path around) while the things that come at you take agent and part names that signal movement. And the register splits along the game's politics: feral organisms get blunt harm-or-part names, the two enforcement classes keep the bureaucratic cover, so Naturalizers and Redactors stand apart as the ones who speak in euphemism while the rest are named for exactly what they do.

## Four renames bring the biology-Latin holdouts onto the harm-naming scheme

| Old name | New name | Pattern | Reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| Techos | **Sapscraps** | harm-verb fused with a condition noun | *sap* (it drains iron and saps the infrastructure) fused with *scraps* (the picked-clean detritus it leaves, and the small scrap-sized bodies of the swarm) |
| Verdings | **Ferrules** | the part you see, on an iron root | *ferr-* (iron) fused with *ferrule*, a reinforcing ring or sleeve — what the C-shaped body clamped at a breach reads as; the sound also faintly echoes *feral*, matching the abandoned service animal |
| Neutros | **Flares** | the effect it leaves | the area burst is a flare, and *flare* reads doubly as an inflammatory flare-up, which is what a neutrophil is for |
| Nosomas | **Redactors** | institutional euphemism | *redact* is erase-from-record, the game's information-suppression motif made into a name, and it pairs Redactors with Naturalizers as the two enforcement classes that talk in cover |

The other nine keep their names. Hidras, Crusts, Candids, Meebs, Naturalizers, Gnawers, Spikers, Tanglers, and Toxos already span the same range of sources, so nothing changed there.

## Thirteen threats, each filling a different niche

| **Sapscraps** | swarm fodder, common | catecholate siderophores (enterobactin, *E. coli*); the three-palp C3 body is the molecule's geometry | Trivial alone, a problem massed. Chip damage, and they strip iron from fixtures you might want, so loitering in open infrastructure costs you. | One palp lifts and glows before the clamp. Not worth fighting singly; thin them with any AoE, or pull them off your line with a Flure decoy. |

| **Hidras** | infrastructure ambush, mid | hydroxamate siderophores (desferrioxamine, ferrichrome); the segmented helix is the iron-cage propeller geometry | Sits disguised as cabling and ambush-cuts unscanned movement; nasty as a surprise, ordinary once spotted. | Reveal it first (Seefern light, or Aster's or Tyreg's overlay). | **Gnawers** | pursuit pack, common in Zone 3 | gingipains (*P. gingivalis*), heme-pigmented proteases tied to Alzheimer's | Packs of two to four that hunt by metabolic signature, so loud or active play draws them. | **Spikers** | delayed line-of-sight connection turret, static | hyperexcitable pyramidal neurons; Alzheimer's network hyperexcitability and seizure-like firing | Rooted. It locks onto movement, establishes a visible connection, and deals damage only if that connection retains clear line of sight for the full authored delay. Any LOS break severs it immediately and cancels the damage. A completed discharge also draws the Tanglers that hunt it. | One branch brightens, then a visible filament and traveling charge show the lock and its progress. | **Tanglers** | stealth grappler, mid | tau pathology; paired helical filaments that spread prion-like and prefer hyperexcitable neurons | Creeps in, grapples with uncoiling hooks, drains you, and leaves a lingering tau status on anyone who survives. | **Flares** | triggered bomb, mid | neutrophils; a multi-lobed nucleus and three granule classes, the burst being collateral oxidative damage | Inert until set off, then a short bright wind-up and an area burst that hits friend and foe. Careless fire and bunching set it off, and the savvy play is triggering it yourself (Myke's flame) into a group. | The membrane swells and brightens for two or three seconds. Leave the radius, do not cluster near one, or pop it on purpose. |
| **Naturalizers** | enforcement patrol, elite | NK cells, with cytotoxic granules deployed at a focused contact synapse | The tag check. Walks a fixed route scanning for incoherent tags and lands a lethal contact-strike on whatever it flags, predictable but deadly within reach. | Granules pack toward one bright point before the strike. Avoid the scan, break to cover, route through Candid air where its scan fails, or pass behind a Gasafoetida pod. |
| **Redactors** | invisible enforcer, late elite | cloaked pathological T-cells using molecular mimicry (cf. *Trypanosoma*), NK-derived kin of the Naturalizers | The late-game stealth threat. Everything healthy hunts it, which you can use. | The conoid extends before a feeble pierce. Easy to kill, easy to ignore, best used as bait the other enemies will chase. |

## Why it reads as a roster, not thirteen damage sponges

The niches do not overlap: fodder (Sapscraps), two flavors of ambush (Ferrules at the chokepoint, Hidras in the cabling), two kinds of terrain (Crusts on the walls, Candids on the floor), two pursuers (Meebs slow, Gnawers in packs), a turret (Spikers), a grappler (Tanglers), a bomb (Flares), two enforcers (Naturalizers patrolling, Redactors cloaked), and one opportunist (Toxos). Every threat answers to a different slice of the kit: fire clears the terrain colonies and arms the bomb; light and overlay defeat the two hidden threats; cover beats the line-of-sight ones; stun buys a window against the pursuers and the grappler; the Flure decoy and the Gasafoetida repellent route you past the iron-seekers and the patrol; and the ecosystem itself is a counter you can aim, baiting Spikers and Tanglers into each other, leading anything into a Candid zone, or lighting a Redactor and letting the room finish it. Most encounters are a routing-and-tools puzzle, not a fight won by trading hits.
