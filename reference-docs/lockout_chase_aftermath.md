# Lockout, Chase, and Aftermath — Scene Dialogue

Scene-level dialogue script for the Act 1 climax: the simulation-boundary checkpoint, the rejection of Aster's and Peris's tags, the Naturalizer chase back to Endo's corridor, and the aftermath at the boundary line. This is the scene where every setup from the earlier part of Act 1 lands its cost. Aster's return-to-simulation goal, Peris's already-fractured wellness framing, Aster's institutional confidence in the system, Endo's accumulating knowledge of what the Naturalizers do. All of it comes due here.

Placement: directly following `residential_rings_dialogue.md`. The Rings closing narration ends with the party walking toward the checkpoint visible at the end of the next corridor, its indicators green, its arch structure clean. This scene picks up in that corridor approach. Act 1, shelter 7 vicinity. The chase drives the party back through the corridors they crossed since Endo's junction, ending at the boundary where Endo's unmaintained section begins.

See also:
- `residential_rings_dialogue.md` — immediately preceding scene, ends with Endo's departure at the junction at the edge of the Rings and the party continuing without him toward the checkpoint
- `endo_junction_dialogue.md` — establishes Aster's return-to-simulation goal and Peris's wellness-framing. Both come due at the checkpoint.
- `stacks_anxiety_scene.md` — establishes "us" as Aster's frame for the shared goal, "focus on what matters" as the phrase Peris uses to steady herself
- `mother_flure_dialogue.md` — the chamber exit establishes the close-to-facility read Aster carries into this scene
- GDD section on the lockout/chase (current lines 1818-1850) — mechanical structure and two anchor lines: Aster's "It's not letting me back in" and Peris's "...Back in to what?"

Style notes per the project conventions:
- No em dashes
- Aster's register moves from institutional-procedural confidence through disbelief into something he does not yet have a word for. He does not yell. He does not break down. His frame breaks in real time and he keeps reaching for the procedural explanation longer than he should.
- Peris's register is strangely quiet through this scene. The Marco encounter already broke her wellness-framing; the checkpoint rejection is the system making explicit what her body already knew. She is not surprised. She is waiting for Aster to catch up.
- Endo is not in this scene as an active character. He departed at the Residential Rings junction (per the preceding scene). His workshop is visible at a distance during the aftermath beat, but he does not come to the party and they do not reach him. His absence is load-bearing: the boundary he maintained saves the party without his being present to save them. Peris is the one who has to physically move Aster out of the rejection, and she is the one who navigates the chase through sensory reading.
- The chase is gameplay-register, not cutscene. Dialogue during the sprint is minimal and physical.

Scene plays in this order:

1. Approach and the first rejection
2. Escalation
3. The Naturalizers activate
4. The chase (gameplay)
5. Boundary standoff
6. The aftermath

## 4. The chase (gameplay)

# Pacing notes for the implementation team

**Register across the scene:**

Aster's arc: confident → confused → procedural-defensive → recognizing → pulled → breathless → flat → bewildered → seated. The register compresses across the scene. He starts verbose (walking Peris through the plan) and ends one-syllable (the trailing "I..."). His frame does not break dramatically; it is incrementally refused, in six distinct moments across the scene, each one peeling back another layer of institutional-procedural confidence. The final image of him sitting on the corridor floor is the frame having reached its limit. He has not yet found a new frame. He is in the gap.

Peris's arc: quiet → calling his name → factual → physically moving Aster → navigating the chase → genuinely asking → reading Endo → offering the phrase back. Her register is the scene's stabilizer. She has already done this collapse; she is the one who has already been rejected by an institution. She is a step ahead of Aster through the whole scene, not because she is calmer by nature but because her collapse happened earlier. The scene lets her be the steady one, which is the role reversal from the Stacks scene. She was the one panicking then; he was the one explaining. Now he is the one the frame is breaking around; she is the one who has to get him out of the corridor and through the chase and back across the boundary. Every escalation of her role is earned by her having gotten there first.

Endo's arc: not present in this scene. He departed at the Residential Rings. His workshop is visible at a distance during the aftermath, continuing his normal work. The scene plays without him as an active character. His absence is felt throughout: Peris has to fill his role as mover and navigator, and the party reaches his boundary without him being at the boundary to receive them.

**Voice direction for the key lines:**

- "Almost there. I'll log the incident, you stay close to me, we're through this in a few minutes." — institutional-procedural confidence, protective, the last moment of the frame as intact
- "That's a glitch. Hold on." — procedural disbelief, not panic
- "...My authorization is revoked." — flat, quiet, the first recognition
- "Aster." — one word from Peris, restraint as the line's weight
- "Aster, it's not going to stop reading me." — factual, calm, Peris naming what she already knows from Marco
- "You can't. Not right now. Come on. Aster, come on." — level, firm, the rhythm of a person moving another person
- "Left. The water sounds clearer." — fast, certain, sensory-register navigation call during the chase
- "Wait. Wait, I can fix this, Peris, I just need to —" — dragged, sentence-interrupted-by-movement, pleading with the person moving him
- "It's not letting me back in." — the GDD anchor line, flat, weight in what is not said
- "...Back in to what?" — the GDD anchor response, genuinely asked, therapist-register, softly
- "...I..." — one syllable, honest arrival at blank
- "He's just... working." — bewildered, not judgmental, looking at Endo in the distance
- "This is just his Tuesday." — small, observational, Peris translating Endo for Aster
- "Aster... do you have a word for this? For how much the situation is different from what it's supposed to be?" — quiet, curious, asking for vocabulary after a long pause
- "Fugacity." — flat, automatic retrieval, real chemistry term (from *fugere*, to flee)
- "Though... technically it's for properties like pressure. Not for situations like this." — Aster's honesty-reflex narrowing the term's applicability
- "Well. I'm under a lot of pressure right now." — the small word-joke, delivered quietly not for laughs but as Peris's translation move
- "...Ha." — a small huff of almost-laughter, the first sound of its kind from Aster since the simulation
- "Focus on what matters, right?" — very quiet, returning the phrase, the scene's quiet payoff

**What the scene does:**

- Lands the Act 1 climax at its intended emotional scale: the return-to-simulation goal that Aster articulated at Endo's junction, that he referenced at Mother Flure ("we're only a few corridors out from the facility approach"), that he narrated in the approach, is refused by the system in three escalating steps (panel reject, hack reject, Peris's tag flag)
- Reverses the Stacks scene's roles: Peris the stabilizer, Aster the one whose frame is breaking. The reversal is earned — she has had her collapse at Marco; now it is his turn.
- Pays off "focus on what matters" as a two-way translation pattern. Aster gave the phrase to Peris at the Stacks; she gives it back here. The relationship is the translation.
- Honors Endo's prior departure at the Rings. The scene is the first real test of the game's promise that Endo's choice to go home was his choice, not a plot convenience. He does not come back to rescue the party. The boundary he maintained does his work for him while he is doing his other work at the junction. The scene refuses to make Endo's absence either a betrayal or a dramatic reveal.
- Establishes the post-lockout emotional landscape. Aster sits on the floor. He has not found a new frame. Peris sits beside him. The rest of Act 1 (shelters 7-8) and early Act 2 will be the process of what they do in the gap.
- Pays off the Naturalizer banter from Tag Day as a recurring ambient horror. The player who remembers that scene hears the banter during the chase and connects the two events: these are the same workers, doing the same job, making the same small game of it.

**What the scene does not do:**

- Does not resolve anything. Aster is not going to have a new frame by the end of this scene. He is going to sit on the floor with Peris and absorb. The resolution, such as it is, happens incrementally across the following shelters.
- Does not have Aster cry, yell, or collapse dramatically. The scene's thesis is the quietness of institutional rejection. The Naturalizers did not drag him away; they stood at a boundary and waited. His frame did not explode; it was simply refused, one handshake at a time. Dramatic breakdown would overclaim the moment. Aster sitting on the floor saying "...I..." is correct.
- Does not have Peris gloat, vindicate, or explain. She has the right to none of those. She simply sits beside him and returns his phrase. The scene refuses the temptation to let her have the "I told you so" beat. She does not tell him so. She receives him, same as he received her.
- Does not make Endo the emotional center. Endo is not the scene's protagonist; the scene is about Aster and Peris. His distant presence at the junction is an environmental note, not a thesis. The scene is not asking him to re-enter the party or deliver closure.
- Does not explain the institutional apparatus in dialogue. Neither character says "the system has rejected us because we are non-compliant." Neither character analyzes what just happened. The analysis is the player's. The characters are too close to it to articulate.
- Does not introduce the post-lockout survival frame. The scene ends with the characters sitting; the next scene begins with them having moved. The implied-time gap between the end of this scene and the beginning of the next is the processing beat the scene refuses to narrate.

**Callbacks and setup:**

- "Almost there. I'll log the incident, you stay close to me..." callbacks to Aster's plan at Endo's junction ("report what happened, get reinstated, we're back in the simulation by tomorrow night"). The exact plan, stated here in its last confident moment, is what the next lines will refuse.
- "Aster, it's not going to stop reading me" callbacks to Peris's wellness-framing breaking at Marco. Peris has been in the "the system will not stop" register since the Rings. The checkpoint is her vindication without her needing to claim it.
- "...Back in to what?" callbacks to every moment in the preceding scenes where Peris and Aster articulated what they were going back to. The drink machine, the milestones, the curmoric, the mentally-recover-then-return-to-clients. Every one of those phrases becomes the answer to Peris's question that Aster cannot speak.
- "Focus on what matters, right?" is the direct callback to the Stacks anxiety scene. The phrase is now a shared vocabulary between them. It sets up the translation-pattern as the relationship's core mechanic.
- The fugacity/pressure exchange is the same translation-pattern played in comic register. Aster offers a precise technical term; Peris uses it elastically; Aster's small laugh is the pattern working. The comic version and the serious version ("focus on what matters") sit adjacent in the scene because both are the relationship doing its work. The player sees the pattern twice in close succession, in two registers, and carries the recognition forward. It also plants "fugacity" as a word the game can return to later — Aster naming the gap between ideal and observed is a thread that can be pulled in Acts 2 and 3 as the gaps compound.
- The Naturalizer banter during the chase callbacks to Tag Day. The Tag Day scene offered the banter as a worldbuilding grace note; this scene reuses it as horror. Same workers, same game, now applied to the party.
- Aster's sitting on the floor at the end sets up the post-lockout survival scenes (shelters 7-8). The next scene's opening beat can show Aster having gotten up, which gives the gap its weight.
- Endo's visible-but-distant workshop figure callbacks to his departure at the Residential Rings. When they came in, Endo was with them; they are coming out without him, and his distance is the scene's most concrete statement of what the lockout has done to their situation. When the party eventually chooses whether to convince him to leave his junction (later in Act 1 or into Act 2), this scene is what they will remember: the first time they came to his boundary alone, and the boundary held.

**On the Naturalizer horror:**

The Naturalizers are scary throughout the scene not because they are violent but because they are calm. They do not run. They do not threaten. They stand at the boundary and wait. Voice direction and animation should lean into the polite-institutional aesthetic — clean uniforms, proper posture, quiet scan-tones, no aggression. The horror is that they do not need aggression. The system works through them without requiring their investment. This is the banality of the institutional violence the game is ultimately about.

The Chelator-mediated slow-down during the chase is thematically consistent: the Naturalizers hesitate near iron-feeding fauna because their protocol has a rule about it. Their hesitation is not cowardice; it is compliance. The party uses institutional compliance as a tactical ally, which is one of the game's repeated moves.

**On Endo's distant presence:**

Endo is not a character in this scene. He is a figure visible at the far end of the corridor, doing his work, not coming to the party. This is exactly right. The scene is not trying to make Endo's absence a statement; the scene is showing what a boundary someone else maintains looks like when it saves you while the maintainer is doing the next thing. The party has not been rescued. They have survived because of infrastructure someone else tended. This is the same thesis the Mother Flure chamber gave the player: care-work as infrastructure. The boundary is Endo's tended thing. It just saved them. He is attending to the next tending-task. The scene trusts the player to see this without having it said.

## Open design questions

- Whether Endo's distant workshop figure should register any awareness of the party when they arrive at the boundary. Options: he glances toward the boundary once, briefly, then resumes work (subtle acknowledgment), or he continues his work without any change (pure routine). Default: the latter. His acknowledgment is the boundary having held, not an eye-contact beat.
- Whether Aster's device should display any final message as he sits on the floor. Options: blank screen, the rejection message still displayed, a cycling set of his old simulation-era notifications that he can no longer access. Worth exploring.
- Whether the Chelators-as-chase-obstacle beat should recur later in the game as a learned tactic. If the party later deliberately routes enemies through Chelator clusters to slow them, the callback lands. If it does not recur, it remains a one-off.
- Whether Peris's "Focus on what matters, right?" should have a small physical beat between the words and Aster's next reaction (if any). A hand on his knee, a breath, or just the line hanging. Default: line hanging, no touch yet. Physical contact between Aster and Peris can be saved for a later scene where it can be earned specifically.
- Whether the scene ends on the narration or adds one more beat. Default: narration closes, the scene cuts to the post-lockout survival opener. A breath longer would be welcome if the production can hold the pause.
