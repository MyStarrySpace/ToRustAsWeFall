# Channels scene: edits to make

Working document tracking revisions to the Channels scene. Each beat notes what stays, what gets cut, what gets rewritten, and why.

## Scene-level changes

Original scene tried to do too much: environmental hazard tutorial + Peris's tending discovery + corpse harvest + ethical exchange + shelter arrival, all in one sequence. Revised scene focuses on the two beats that matter: introducing Peris's memory mechanic and the corpse-harvest ethical beat. Flora tending moves to a later scene.

Environmental hazard lesson (flow vs stagnant) was already taught in the bridge/junction sequences. Remove the redundancy. Don't re-tutorial what the player already knows.

### Rewrite
- `channels.aster.fluid` → "Flow rate correlates with atmospheric quality. Don't quote me on the mechanism."
  - One clause instead of three. "Don't quote me" characterizes Aster as methodologically scrupulous and sets up the running joke where he flags his own reach.
- `channels.peris.sound` → "So, just go with the flow?"
  - Delivered as Endo starts walking. Question form, not declaration. Acknowledges Aster's hedge by responding in kind. "So," is Peris's social-worker reflection tic bleeding into casual conversation.

```
Narration: The corridor opens. Water runs along the floor in shallow channels, cleaner where it flows.

```

## Beat 2: Memory recognition (replaces the stagnant-pool environmental beat)

This beat is now the introduction of Peris's memory overlay mechanic. The Channels are where the party encounters the bodies they saw from above during the bridge sequence. Peris remembers them in full detail. She leads the group to the first body. This establishes a baseline for her memory capacity that the game will measure degradation against for the rest of the runtime.

### Cut entirely
- `channels.endo.stop` — redundant, environmental hazards already taught.
- `channels.aster.stagnant` — redundant, and the "iron concentration spikes" line is data-dump exposition.
- `channels.peris.thick` — writerly for Peris's voice, and the biofilm air lesson isn't needed.

### Add new staging and dialogue
```
[Peris's memory overlay activates as they enter the space. Blurry shapes at first — but they resolve into sharp, specific memories: bodies she saw through the grating from the bridge. Small details visible: a posture, a color, a hand turned a particular way. The memory is vivid.]

Peris: I know this place.
Aster: We haven't been down here.
Peris: No. But I saw it.

[Peris walks ahead of the others. She goes directly to where the first body is, without hesitation. Aster and Endo follow.]
```

### Critical design notes for this beat
- The sharpness of Peris's memory is the baseline the game will measure her future degradation against. Small details (posture, clothing, specifics) matter here because their absence will register later.
- Peris leads the group using her memory. Do not have Aster or Endo comment on this. The player needs to notice it peripherally, so that when she later follows the group instead of leading, the asymmetry lands.
- Do not overplay the memory reveal. The scene establishes the capacity; later scenes vary against it. Restraint now so devastation later.

### Later payoff (reference for future scenes — NOT part of Channels edits)
- A later bridge-to-below scene mirrors this one. Peris sees bodies from above, then they go below. The bodies are blurs, not sharp. She doesn't say "I know this place." She follows the group instead of leading.
- If revisiting the Channels late-game, even the originally-sharp memories have degraded to blurs. The baseline has been eroded.
- Optional hard beat: Peris asks "Do you know this place?" and Aster answers "Yes. You do too. You led us here last time."

## Beat 3: The body and the harvest

### Keep
- `channels.narration.body` — doing its job.
- `channels.peris.people` — keep exactly.
- `channels.aster.hungry` — keep exactly. Do not touch.

### Rewrite/add

`channels.endo.kneel` → "Endo eats." Cut the cinematographic staging ("practiced, efficient," "His hands move. He stands with something in his palm."). Blunt matches Endo's register.

`channels.peris.what` → cut. Replaced by Peris's sensory/memory reaction below.

`channels.aster.nutrients` → cut. Replaced by Aster's sitcom-foodie deflection.

New lines to add:

- **Aster**: "I'm still going to file that report, by the way." — Callback to bridge scene ("all casualties are supposed to be logged"). Delivered as Endo is eating. The "by the way" and "still" do the work: Aster re-committing to procedure in the middle of procedure-destroying reality. He's watching the report become nonsense and filing it anyway. Stubbornness or faith or denial; player decides.
- **Peris**: "Ugh, the smell..." — Physical recoil, vocalized. Peris says "ugh" because she's a person who says "ugh." The horror isn't the reaction; it's what she's reacting to.
- **Peris**: "...this is what my clients were eating?" — Past progressive ("were eating") does real work. Not a completed past action but an ongoing condition she was on the other side of. The possessive ("my clients") activates the care relationship the portal mediated. See portal-mediation note below.
- **Aster**: "So, I've heard you guys eat lysate, huh? Time to expand my palate." — Sitcom-foodie deflection. Aster coping by importing the language of restaurant reviews into corpse-eating. "You guys" marks the threshold: he's addressing the worker class he's about to join as a visiting outsider, a fiction he maintains for exactly one sentence. Also deploys the vocabulary split — Aster uses "lysate."
- **Aster**: "Hey, this is a big downgrade for me too..." — Added after "and we're hungry." The "hey" reaches for Peris tonally. "Big downgrade" is consumer-comparison register, the language of a life where things got better or worse and you had a standard of living. Aster admitting his own loss in the only vocabulary he has for loss. The trailing "..." lets Peris receive the line as either humor or honesty.

### Final shape
```
Narration: A figure in the drainage channel. Still. The water flows around them. They stopped moving a long time ago.
[Endo eats.]
Aster: I'm still going to file that report, by the way.
Peris: Ugh, the smell...
Peris: ...this is what my clients were eating?
Aster: So, I've heard you guys eat lysate, huh? Time to expand my palate.
Peris: These were people.
Aster: They were. And we're hungry.
Aster: Hey, this is a big downgrade for me too...
```

### Design notes for this beat

**Beat density.** Nine beats in rapid succession. Earned by the scene's thematic load, but the staging must give them breathing room. Narration beats between dialogue lines, pauses for player absorption, slowed character animation during Peris's recoil. The dialogue is dense; the staging should not be.

**The scene's emotional arc.** Aster's procedural reflex fires → Endo acts → Peris has a bodily reaction → Peris's mind catches up → Aster deflects into sitcom → Peris names the weight → Aster meets it pragmatically → Aster admits his own loss. Tonal counterpoint throughout: sitcom surface, horror underneath, warmth in the characters trying to meet each other across their different registers.

### Portal-mediation note

Peris's recoil at the smell is the physical discovery that the portal (her work medium) never carried scent. She saw her clients eating lysate for years. She never smelled it. The system's mediation layer filtered out sensory information that would have made professional composure impossible. She's now encountering what was always on the other side of that filter.

This is the same realization that "the feed was a wall the whole time" lands later (Residential Rings). The Channels scene is the visceral version, happening earlier in the game. Peris's professional composure was partly propped up by the system's filtering; outside the system, the composure is thinner than she knew.

Do not name the portal mechanism in dialogue. The word "clients" and the tense shift ("were eating") let the player infer it.

### Vocabulary split

Lysate / starch is the same substance under two different class vocabularies. UI reflects this: when Aster, Peris, or Tyreg is selected, the substance shows as "Lysate." When Endo, Myke, or Oli is selected, it shows as "Starch."

- **Lysate** (trained-class): Peris (care-professional), Aster (data-analyst), Tyreg (academy-enforcement). Clinical, Greek-rooted, medicalized. Lets the speaker refer to the substance without acknowledging what it came from.
- **Starch** (worker-class): Endo (wall-maintenance), Myke (infrastructure), Oli (insulation). Common, simple, unmarked. The word anyone has for what they eat.

The vocabulary is biography. Every "lysate" or "starch" a character speaks displays their origin.

Later-game implications (reference for future work):
- Character switching vocabularies marks an arc beat. Tyreg saying "starch" means something has changed for her. Peris shifting from "lysate" to "starch" marks her migration from professional register to worker register.
- The Inflammashunt puzzle's three-route information gathering can use the split: Aster's terminal route speaks "lysate"; Myke's crawlspace route speaks "starch." The vocabulary split is part of what makes the routes feel like different layers of the same system.
- The faction ruin: surviving signage/logs using only "starch" implies the trained class was wiped out.
- The cure creator: trained-class background, but their archive might show a drift from "lysate" to "starch" over time, marking their alienation from the system that trained them.
- Peris's memory degradation: does she lose "lysate" first (professional self goes) or "starch" first (embodied connection goes)? Either is cruel in a specific way. Hold in reserve.

## Beat 3.5: The second ferrolure (flora tending)

After the harvest beat, as the party moves toward the shelter, they pass another ferrolure. Dormant, or partial, or half-grown. Not a tactical encounter this time. Peris goes to it automatically because that's what she does with plants.

### Purpose

This beat is the quiet coda to the body scene. Peris has just been through the realization that the portal filtered her clients' reality from her, and that her hands are now part of a survival act she used to only witness. The ferrolure is the answer the scene gives her: her tending capacity still works. The same hands that recoiled from the lysate can still make something grow. Care survives the shock.

The beat also reframes the Channels zone retroactively. The player thought it was drainage infrastructure. By scene's end, it's a place where things are still growing if someone looks for them. That's Peris's gift and the game's thesis in one mechanic.

### Aster's alarm

Aster has just seen what the ferrolure does: lure → siderophores → flow → clear. That's his working model after Beat 3. When Peris walks up to another one, his model fires and his first reaction is alarm, not admiration. He tries to stop her by naming the object's function.

This also rhymes structurally with Beat 1, where Aster hedged on correlation and Peris cut through with the pun. Same rhythm again: Aster over-analyzes, Peris acts, Aster updates. Two parallel micro-exchanges that establish their working dynamic across the scene.

### Peris's practical assessment becomes philosophical

Peris's response to Aster's alarm is a practical safety assessment: the lure is emitting, but nothing nearby is receiving the signal, so it's inert. That's the immediate-situation meaning. It also describes her professional life (emitting care through a portal to clients processed as signals by a mediation system), the workers' condition (maintaining infrastructure for absent users), and the game's whole thesis in one sentence.

The pause after she says it is the beat. She says something practical and hears herself say something else. She doesn't explain the parallel. The pause is the line, structurally. A player tracking themes will feel it land. A player who isn't will still feel Peris catch herself.

Then she touches the ferrolure. The act of tending happens in the full weight of what she just realized, which changes its meaning: she's not touching the plant in innocence, she's touching it as an act of response. The lure was emitting to nothing. Peris arrives. The lure's emission now has a receiver. Her hands on it is the meeting of signal and receiver after a long silence on both sides.

### Dialogue

From the original scene, reused here:

- `channels.peris.touch` → trim to "It responds to me."
- `channels.peris.always` → keep. "They always have" now works on multiple levels: a thing she still has, her correcting Aster's alarm, and the continuation of the response-pattern she just observed the ferrolure to lack.

New lines:

- **Aster (before she touches it)**: "That's a lure." — Compressed alarm disguised as information-delivery.
- **Peris**: "Nothing's around to receive its signals." — Practical assessment that turns philosophical as she hears it. The pause after this line is load-bearing; hold it.

### Final shape

```
Narration: Something is growing where the water seeps into the wall. A ferrolure. Dormant. Peris is already walking toward it.
Aster: That's a lure.
Peris: Nothing's around to receive its signals.
Peris: ...
[She touches the ferrolure. It responds.]
Peris: It responds to me.
Peris: Plants just... respond to me. They always have.
```

The "..." is a Peris dialogue line, not a cinematic staging beat. The pause is her turn to speak, used to not-speak. Character action, not game staging. Players click through at their own pace; the beat lasts exactly as long as each player wants. Use existing style tags (normal or fragment) rather than inventing a new one.

### Aster's closing response

Optional. The scene may have done enough work that it can end on Peris's "they always have" and let the shelter narration carry the transition. If Aster speaks, the previous draft's "Okay. Noted." still works as a character-grounding note. My lean: no closing line. The pause did the work. Let the beat end on Peris.

### Endo's response

He already knew she could do this. Quiet recognition, maybe a small nod, probably nothing. Endo stays in his mode.

### Transition to shelter

Peris steps away from the ferrolure. The cutscene ends.

## Beat 3.75: Hide-and-run sequence

Control returns from the cutscene to Endo. On his overlay, a hide spot is now highlighted, along with the two ferrolures and the exit. Between the party and the exit, a siderophore cluster of seven entities blocks the main corridor. Their collective detection radius covers the corridor width, so the party cannot sneak around them even if the cluster is fixated on a single lure. The puzzle requires a two-step lure sequence to draw the cluster off the path, with the party hiding in an occluded notch during the migration window.

See `channels_hide_encounter_spec.md` for the full puzzle spec, including corridor layout, siderophore behavior, detection radii, stamina math, intended solution path, and failure modes.

### Purpose

Teaches the hide mechanic and the two-ferrolure coordination pattern that the game will reuse in later zones. Introduces another layer of Endo's overlay: he doesn't just know where shelters and tools are; he knows where to hide. Each layer of his overlay reinforces his characterization as the party's survival guide through offered knowledge rather than spoken instruction.

Also does pacing work. After the quiet philosophical beat of the second ferrolure, the player needs a mode shift back into tactical engagement before the shelter ends the scene. The hide-and-run gives that shift without requiring a new location.

### Mechanic summary

- Ferrolures activate on hold-interact (2s hold).
- Two ferrolures are needed because a single active lure doesn't clear the corridor; the cluster swarms the lure at a distributed radius that still blocks passage.
- Lure 1 fixates the cluster. Peris retreats to lure 2 and activates it while the cluster is still fixated on lure 1. Peris joins the hide. Lure 1 expires and the cluster migrates past the hide toward lure 2. The hide's panel occludes line of sight; siderophores pass by without detecting the party. Once the cluster is at lure 2, the path to the exit is clear.
- Hide spots are environmental features with panel occlusion. Character behind the panel is out of line of sight; fixated-state detection does not penetrate occlusion.
- Stamina management is the key resource challenge: the retreat distance exceeds Peris's max-run budget at full stamina, so she must walk part of the retreat, which creates the tension window against lure 1's expiration.

### Planning visualization

When the player enters planning mode (paused state), the overlay shows the proposed sequence: character positions, both ferrolure locations, hide spot, exit door, with arrows and timing indicators for each stage. Diegetic justification: Endo gestures (points at lure 1, points at lure 2, points at hide, points at exit) and the UI visualizes what he's indicating. The gesture-triggers-UI pattern gives the mechanical planning warmth without making it slow, and it scales to later sequences that will require more complex plans.

### Pedagogy

First hide-and-run is hand-held: planning visualization is available, Endo points out the sequence, the timing window is forgiving (lure 1 duration 10s, lure 2 duration 13s at design-point parameters). The shortcut back to Endo's junction is unlocked before this encounter (flora gate from Beat 3.5 opens the shortcut), so failure means cheap retreat and retry. Later encounters in other zones will assume the player knows the activation-hide-run pattern and will ask them to combine it with other mechanics without the tutorializing.

### Structural notes

The Channels scene is now long (roughly 20-30 minutes with six beats). First zones are allowed to be long because they teach, but the emotional beats (body/harvest, second ferrolure) need to feel distinct from the tactical beats (first ferrolure, hide-and-run) so the player's mode-of-attention shifts cleanly. Likely means lighting, score, or camera framing differences between tactical and dialogue segments, so the player's body knows what kind of beat they're in.

## Endo control switch and ferrolure sequence

Somewhere in the Channels, the player gets control of Endo for the first time. Probably before the body/harvest beat, so the player has experienced him as a party member first and as a controllable character second. The switch introduces his map overlay and establishes a mechanical pattern (environmental rhythm + hold-on-interactable) that the game will reuse.

### The control switch

When control shifts to Endo, his overlay becomes visible. It is visually distinct from Aster's data overlay and Peris's memory overlay. The ferrolure is highlighted on it. The player retroactively understands how Endo has been navigating: he has always been reading his own overlay, and his gestures (pointing at walls, stopping at pools, leading to shelters) have been him sharing his information with the party in the only way he can.

Do not tutorialize this. Let the player figure it out by being him.

### The wave

When Endo moves toward the ferrolure, if no action is queued, he faces Peris and waves at her.

The wave is information-sharing as affection. Endo knows the ferrolure is a plant, knows what it does, knows siderophores will come for it. Peris is the party member who tends living things. He is calling her attention to a situation in her domain. That is also how Endo expresses affection, because Endo expresses affection only by doing things that mean things. The gesture holds both meanings simultaneously and the player can read it as either; both are correct.

Peris waves back automatically. Two characters, one exchange, no dialogue, visible only to players who are watching closely enough to pause and see it. This is a reward for attention.

### The ferrolure-flow mechanic

The ferrolure attracts siderophores. A channel flow runs in a consistent rhythm the player can learn. When flow surges arrive, siderophores drawn to the lure are washed out.

The player can read the flow cycle through three redundant cues:
- Water level rises before a surge
- VFX on the channel during flow
- Water sounds louder as flow builds

Different players will latch onto different cues. Redundancy makes the rhythm legible without UI indicators.

### The scripted first encounter

First encounter uses wide channels, so the flow guarantees siderophore washout without tight timing. The player sees the full lure-attract-wash cycle work under safe conditions and learns the mechanic's shape. Harder encounters later narrow the channels, compress the flow window, or introduce overlapping rhythms.

### The hold-on-interactable design

Activating the ferrolure requires the player to click and stand at the interactable for a set duration. The flow rhythm is consistent enough that the player can start the hold early and finish standing when the surge arrives. This turns the mechanic into rhythm-reading rather than reaction-timing.

The hold requirement also creates real-time space with nothing to do, which is an opportunity for small staging: a line of dialogue, a character beat, an environmental detail. Design should use these windows, not fight them.

### Indirect solutions as default

If the player finds the timing hard, the game's other systems compensate:
- Character switching spreads timing across multiple actors in position
- Ability synergies (Protect, EMP, Conduct, Inflame) modify the encounter conditions
- Pause-and-queue lets the player plan precise sequences from a safe state
- Route choice allows avoiding siderophore crossings where possible
- Environmental reading (the three cues above) extends the effective reaction window

Direct intervention is expensive by design. The game rewards setup over reflex. This is thematically congruent: care work is infrastructural, not heroic. You can't swoop in at the right moment; you have to have been there beforehand.

### Thematic note

Learning the Channels' rhythms is how Peris (and the player) start to become the kind of person who can survive out here. Endo already reads the rhythms. Peris's tending instinct is about to extend from plants to environmental systems. The scene is quietly teaching the player that care and attention are survival skills, not soft skills.
