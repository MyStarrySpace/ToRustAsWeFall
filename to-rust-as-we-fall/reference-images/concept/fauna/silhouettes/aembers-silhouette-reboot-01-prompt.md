# Aembers Silhouette Reboot 01

## Design basis

The Aember is a mid-tier breach-camping hazard rather than a chasing enemy.
It was originally cultivated as a living vascular-breach detector and iron
wick. Its fluorescent core exposed a leak while its soft anchoring tissue
sealed or stabilized the damaged interface. After abandonment, it remained at
rich breach sites and began treating warm iron-bearing bodies as replacement
sources.

The player-facing prediction is:

> If I linger in the lane it guards, its core will track toward me, the body
> will draw taut, and it will flare in that direction. Breaking sight,
> crossing during its relaxed beat, or collapsing it with water interrupts
> that sequence.

## Generation prompt

```text
Use case: stylized-concept
Asset type: early silhouette exploration sheet for a mid-tier enemy in a stylized low-poly dark science-fiction game
Primary request: Create six genuinely different, simple silhouette ideas for the Aember, an abandoned living vascular-breach detector and iron wick that has become a stationary ambush hazard. It camps ruptures and narrow crossings; it does not chase. When a warm iron-bearing body lingers in its lane, its single fluorescent core tracks toward that body, the creature draws taut around the core, then releases a short directional flare. Water makes its soft anchoring tissue collapse and interrupts the flare.
Scene/backdrop: clean warm gray-off-white paper, no environment, props, borders, labels, or text
Subject: a 2x3 sheet of six separate feral Aember variants at the same scale, each shown in a clear three-quarter silhouette-oriented view. Every variant must look like a damaged biological breach plug or wound seal that tore itself free: a broad anchoring flange, gripping skirt, split clamp-foot, or stretched membrane base supporting one elevated core-bearing mass. Use aggressive asymmetry, strained tension, hooked anchoring edges, sagging torn tissue, and a forward lean to make it read as a hostile territorial hazard without giving it a face. The external outline—not internal texture—must distinguish each idea.
Six shape directions: (1) low wedge with a huge split clamp-foot and a taut arched back; (2) narrow leaning staple-shape planted on two broad anchor pads; (3) flattened three-lobed seal whose central membrane rises like a pulled slingshot; (4) crooked hanging droplet supported between two torn flange legs; (5) crescent body braced by one massive rear anchor and one small forward hook; (6) squat ruptured plug whose skirt has folded into three uneven gripping knuckles. These are shape prompts, not literal machinery.
Core invariant: exactly one small chartreuse cyclic core per creature, a bent ring or folded loop visible as a simple cutout/glow within the mass; never a generic ball and never an eye. Keep the core subordinate to the outer silhouette.
Style/medium: extremely simplified black and smoky dark-olive creature silhouettes with only the single chartreuse core visible; chunky low-poly/voxel-painterly shape language; 5–8 major masses per design; no surface rendering beyond a few broad facets
Composition/framing: six isolated specimens, generous spacing, no overlaps, all fully in frame, same scale, clean 2x3 production concept sheet
Mood: biological, neglected, tense, ugly, alien, and dangerous; no comedy and no cuteness
Constraints: stationary breach-camper anatomy; strong readable external shape; soft tissue plausibly collapses under water; exactly one core; no eyes, faces, mouths, teeth, arms, hands, claws, tentacle forests, insect legs, shells, machinery, tubes, weapons, cute slime forms, generic sea slugs, polished glass, ornate detail, text, labels, numbers, watermark, or environment
```
