# Aembers Origin Exploration 01

## Use

Early silhouette and shape exploration for a mid-tier enemy in a
voxel-painterly dark science-fiction game.

## Origin

Aembers were imported and bred as living breach detectors and iron wicks.
Their soft transparent bodies absorbed ferric seepage from ruptured vessels
while a pyoverdine-inspired internal chromophore fluoresced in proportion to
the leak. Maintenance crews doused, collected, unloaded, and reseated them.

After abandonment, colonies remained attached until starvation forced them to
detach. Their anchor tissue became a creeping foot; their overloaded diagnostic
core became an aggressive iron-attraction organ; and they now mistake warm
moving bodies for fresh leaks.

## Composition

A clean 2x3 exploration sheet on warm gray-off-white paper. Show six separate
present-day feral variants at the same scale in clear three-quarter views,
without labels, text, borders, props, environment, or an action sequence.

## Species invariants

- Small-backpack sized, low to the ground, soft, and slightly elongated.
- A translucent or semi-translucent pale green-yellow gelatinous body.
- One and only one brilliant chartreuse internal chromophore.
- The core is a planar folded loop, bent luminous ring, or cyclic ribbon—not
  an eye or generic glowing ball.
- Surrounding gel diffuses the core into a restrained halo.
- Locomotion uses one broad creeping anchor-foot or a few fused underside
  lobes, like a wound plug that learned to crawl.
- A short feeding wick may be concealed at the front; no toothed mouth.
- Old domestication evidence can include a seating groove, clipped anchor
  edge, maintenance notch, or faded dosing band.
- Feral damage can include sagging gel, asymmetric growth, dried edges,
  ferric contamination, an overloaded core pressing against the membrane, or
  a sealing flange repurposed for crawling.

## Six directions

1. Flattened leaking blister with a suspended loop and puckered anchor skirt.
2. Hunched resin pod with a core hanging from an internal stalk.
3. Crescent-shaped crawling seal with the core visible through the curve.
4. Ruptured vessel plug with an old seating flange and dragging gel foot.
5. Low amphipod-like wedge built from three or four broad gel lobes.
6. Sagging lantern sac with a low core and short front feeding wick.

## Role and style

Mid-tier; encountered in small groups at vessel breaches. Use roughly 6–10
major masses, broad faceted gel planes, one dominant internal core, and
minimal secondary detail.

At idle, the core pulses slowly near center mass. During attack it can brighten
and shift toward the front as a feeding wick extends. Water dousing should
plausibly flatten or interrupt the gel body.

Use production-friendly low-poly or voxel-painterly rendering. Simplify
translucency into chunky layered planes. Palette: pale jaundiced-green gel,
smoky teal-gray contamination, brilliant chartreuse core, ferric-rust
inclusions, and faded maintenance-orange marks.

Avoid Sapscrap anatomy, central mouths, teeth, claws, radial arms, long
tentacles, octopus forms, many insect legs, eyes, faces, cute slimes, glowbug
mascots, polished glass orbs, generic sea slugs, realistic transparency,
ornate machinery, gore, excess tendrils, spikes, wings, labels, and text.
