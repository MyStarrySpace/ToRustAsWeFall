# Sapscraps Tail-Launch Motion 03 — Mutated

## Edit target

`sapscraps-tail-launch-motion-02.png`

Preserve the 2x2 layout, creature anatomy, four poses, and tail-powered action
sequence. Make the creature mutated, hostile, and unsettling instead of neat,
polished, cute, or cartoonish.

## Art direction

- Break the clean symmetry with crooked arm curves, uneven joints, lopsided
  faceted growth, small knuckled bulges, warped planes, and chipped edges.
- Treat the central opening as an unnatural hollow organ or damaged mineral
  aperture: irregular and pinched, with layered torn-looking ridges and deep
  interior shadow. Keep it visibly open in every pose.
- Replace the toy-like finish with dry, rough mineral-organic surfaces: bruised
  plum, black-violet recesses, muted raw-crimson cracks, sparse sickly-magenta
  subsurface glow, soot-dark abrasion, and restrained mottling.
- Render the six donor pads as worn calcified sensory organs: dirty ivory,
  slightly mismatched, chipped or pitted, blunt and functional rather than
  cute toes or teeth.
- Give each pose a harsher gesture: a hunched idle with concealed hooks, a
  tense spasmodic wind-up, a violent predatory launch, and an invasive
  three-way capture.
- Use angular shadows and ominous contrast. Aim for mature dark-science-fiction
  creature concept art with a hand-sculpted corrupted-alien quality while
  retaining a production-readable silhouette.

## Structural invariants

- Exactly three chelating arms, all approximately the same anatomical length
  and thickness.
- One equal arm functions as the support/tail through curvature and pose only.
- Each arm forks into exactly two ivory donor pads, six pads total.
- A clearly hollow central capture cavity remains visible in idle, wind-up,
  and launch.
- During capture, all three arms converge around one rust-red iron sphere with
  all six pads.
- Preserve the sequence: tail-arm supports, compresses, launches the body as
  the other arms swing forward, then whips forward to complete capture.

Avoid mascot proportions, polished toy surfaces, perfect donut geometry,
expressive faces, eyes, mouths, teeth, conventional heads or torsos, gore,
intestines, excessive spikes, extra limbs, unequal arm lengths, a missing
central cavity, labels, arrows, and text.
