# The Director's Design Method

An analysis of how this game's levels actually get designed, distilled from
the design sessions themselves — the wash relay restart, the 2.5-turn spiral,
the center fall, the offshoot dens, the reversible-channel pitch. This is the
method to emulate when composing new content. It is descriptive, not
aspirational: every principle below is cited to a real decision.

## 1. Feeling first, mechanism second

Design starts by naming the emotional beat, then deriving the arrangement
that produces it. "The player is SHOCKED by their initial presence and runs
into conveniently placed tight hiding spots, adding some tension" — the
mechanics (sight-blocking corners, emerging patrols, capbage at the mouth)
were derived backward from shock → panic → relief. Likewise "it feels like
a chore" and "hella boring" were treated as SYSTEMS bugs, never presentation
bugs: the answer to boredom was new interlocking content (the dens), not
polish on what existed.

**Ask:** what should the player FEEL here, second by second? Then: what
arrangement of existing systems produces exactly that curve?

## 2. Compose, never invent

New content is a new ARRANGEMENT of the canon vocabulary — fauna, flora,
channels, hides, the stamina economy, gates, lures — never a new bespoke
mechanism. The offshoot dens are entirely composed: Sapscrap patrols +
sight-blocked geometry + Capbage's tight hide + the salvage-dwell grammar.
When a design genuinely needs a verb the kit lacks (reversible channel flow,
channel-carried debris), the verb is added to the KIT once, proved in its
own fragment, and only then composed into levels. The vocabulary grows
rarely and deliberately; the combinations grow constantly.

**Ask:** which canon systems provide this? If none: is the missing thing a
level's one-off (reject it) or a reusable verb worth teaching the kit?

## 3. One geometry, many readings

Prefer structures where a single shape serves every system at once. The
spiral is simultaneously the level, the curriculum ladder (each lap one
lesson harder), the failure economy (sweeps carry you down through
everything you climbed — the price IS the geometry), the opening spectacle
(fall through the center and the whole climb flashes past), and the
camera's stage. The center-fall correction ("the bridge should be over the
center so they fall straight down") was exactly this instinct: make the
geometry legible in one shot, one motion, one read.

**Ask:** does this shape do one job or five? Corrections tend toward the
CLEANER geometric read — straight over zigzag, center over offset.

## 4. Structural invariants, composed interiors

Repeating skeletons hold everything: a stretch runs shelter → climb →
shelter (the first shelter is home and runback-end; the second connects
onward and owns completion). A section names exactly one verb. A set piece
separates its control from its effect. Offshoots hang off a main spine.
The skeleton never varies; what's composed inside it always does.

## 5. The curriculum gradient

Teach → transfer → exam. Turn one teaches with instruments (the gauge, the
terminal); turn two withholds them ("the political economy IS the
pedagogy" — the instruments end where the maintenance class stopped
caring); turn three asks it all back from memory, darker and faster.
Inversions are the favorite trick: teach that running fails, then build the
span where running is the only answer. Every rung carries a DISCRIMINATOR —
the specific, tested way a player who skipped that lesson fails here, now.

## 6. Information fairness

Tension comes from what you cannot see — but the smart player is never
cheated. The den corner hides the dweller, and the capbage at the mouth is
the visible promise of an answer; the reveal rings draw the actual
detection boundary; the wash's flood edge is the kill edge. "Guessing fails
many ways, but it should be clear if the player is smart." Fear is priced
honestly: every ambush has a tell before it and an answer beside it.

## 7. Loops, not lines

The strongest beats route the player through the SAME space under changing
state: hide → dash past → hide again → return; sweep → runback → sloperope;
lure it out → cross behind it → it comes home. The reversible-channel pitch
is this at full scale — the same ground re-priced by flow direction, debris
position, and stamina, crossed and re-crossed. State changes make known
ground new; new ground is expensive, new STATE is nearly free.

## 8. The world argues for itself

Everything placed answers who built it, what flows through it, and why they
left. The architecture is BUILT, not arranged: decks are held up by
trusses, dens have dead lamps, the camp clings to the outfall on scaffold
legs. The fiction and the difficulty curve are the same object — where
society's maintenance ended, the instruments end, and the level gets
harder. Placement without civic purpose is rejected on sight.

## 9. Audit by watching

Review happens on ARTIFACTS — money shots, recorded playthroughs — hunting
for what should NOT be there and what reads wrong, not verifying that
additions appeared. Corrections arrive as re-framings of the fantasy ("the
actual version has them starting above the spiral, and the bridge falls"),
not parameter tweaks. When something reads wrong the model in his head is
the authority, and the fix is stated as the corrected READ, leaving the
mechanism to be derived.

## 10. Canon is load-bearing

Names, species, behaviors come from the taxonomies and the GDD — never
approximated. A lure only works on siderophores; a tight hide is a Capbage;
retired names are traps. The canon isn't reference material; it is the
parts inventory that makes composition (rule 2) possible at all — you can
only compose what has stable, agreed semantics.

## 11. The hazard is the tool

Rule 3 for FORCES, not just shapes: every force must carry both valences,
reachable by arrangement. The wash sweeps the player, kills the sentry,
drowns the lured watcher, carries the debris that becomes the bridge, and
flushes the dens before a crossing. Design the force, then ask: how does
the player TURN it?

## 12. The player edits the topology

Not lock-and-key — state variables that reconfigure what connects to what,
with remote cause and displaced effect (flip a channel, a crossing
assembles two turns away; raise the water, a bowl becomes three different
maps). The map itself is operable plumbing the player works from inside.

## 13. Braided, not sequenced

Systems share the same ground at the same time: the stealth tempo and the
flow puzzle occupy one space, and progress in one is metered by the other
(the stamina bar as metronome). Never a puzzle room next to a stealth room.

## 14. Round-trip pricing

The body is the token. Costs are paid in return legs under degraded
conditions — run past, then run BACK; cross at mid-water, return at high;
spend the bar going in and owe the dash coming out.

## 15. Instruments sharpen, never gate

The player's natural instinct (watch the water, time it yourself) must be
able to win. Perception reads and charts are amplifiers with real marginal
value, never keys: requirement lives in the body — position, timing,
stealth — not in a menu of reads. A read that adds no decision is a chore.

## 16. Branches trade on distinct axes

Every information/preparation branch must buy something with its own
marginal shape — the Basin's triangle: self-timing is free but priced in
ERROR; the rota chart costs a detour and pays in FORESIGHT (a wide window,
still manually executed); Aster's read costs RESOURCE (stamina/cooldown
from the one closed budget) and pays in PERFECT execution of the next
window only. No branch dominates; every pairing is a real decision; the
free path's failures are the advertisement for the paid ones. And the
tradeoff is legible BEFORE the choice — the chart is visible from the
whole floor, so "narrow window now vs. go earn the timing" is chosen with
open eyes, never tripped over.

---

## The working loop, condensed

1. Name the feeling and its curve.
2. Pick the geometry that serves the most systems at once.
3. Compose canon systems inside the standard skeleton until the curve
   emerges; extend the kit only when a reusable verb is truly missing —
   and prove it in isolation first.
4. Grade it: teach, transfer, exam; give every lesson a discriminator.
5. Price the information: every threat a tell, every panic an answer.
6. Prefer loops over lines; re-price ground with state.
7. Make the world justify every placement.
8. Give every force both valences; let the player turn the hazard.
9. Price the branches on distinct axes; advertise the tradeoff up front;
   let the free path's failures sell the paid ones.
10. Watch it back like a stranger; correct the READ, not the numbers.
