## candid — diverges / rebuild

The canopy — a horizontal overhanging roof on the sheet — is built as six vertical blades standing on end, and the basal lump band is a flat floor decal, so the colony has no mound and none of the sheet's three horizontal strata read.

- **structural.** SHEET: The top stratum is a sparse HORIZONTAL roof: branched filament rosettes lying flat across the whole colony and overhanging the bead columns like eaves. In the silhouette strip it is a thin ragged horizontal slab whose twigs project sideways past the outermost columns on both ends; in the turnaround it caps every view. The written brief agrees: 'a sparse horizontal roof made from a few alpha-cut filament cards.'
  BUILD: build_candid()'s canopy loop calls b.card(..., axis='Y', rot=(0,0,k*1.3+0.5)). Per builder.py, axis='Y' places the quad in the X-Z plane — UPRIGHT — and a Z rotation only yaws it, so all six cards stand on end. They span z=0.11..0.41 on a ring of radius SPAN*0.3=0.51 m inside a 1.7 m colony, so they cover the inner ~40% and overhang nothing. They also wear STRAND_ART, the bead-chain painter, not a branched-filament one. Both renders show them as upright striped slabs — there is no roof anywhere in the piece.
  FIX: In the canopy loop switch to axis='Z' flat cards at one height above the chain tops, and spread them to at least SPAN*0.5 radius so their edges overhang the colonnade. Register a dedicated dendritic painter (radial branching arms with alpha between them, per the sheet's rosettes) instead of reusing STRAND_ART.
- **structural.** SHEET: The substrate band is a RAISED skirt of large fused rounded yeast-cell lumps with real height — it is what lifts the colonnade off the floor, gives the silhouette its continuous lumpy baseline, and makes the whole thing read as a mound.   BUILD: b.card((0,0,0.012), (SPAN, SPAN*0.86), 'cd_carpet', axis='Z', art=CARPET_ART) — one flat quad lying on the floor, zero height, indistinguishable in kind from the bleach stain 8 mm below it. This is the Meeb-cup class of error inverted: a band that holds the FORM has been built as texture.
  FIX: The basal band holds the form, so it is a mesh (house law), while its lump pattern stays drawn. Replace the carpet card with a low faceted dome/berm (Builder ngon prism or a lofted lumpy rim) about 0.12 m tall at centre with a ragged lumpy rim, carrying the existing CARPET_ART as its texture.   FIX: Author the burn as a COLLAPSE, not an erasure — rig.clip's dict form exists for exactly this ({'scale': (1.05, 1.05, 0.05)} flattens the canopy onto the mound while it spreads). For the dormant carpet, add a co-located charred card weighted to a sibling bone that scales up as the live carpet flattens, so the darkening is a rigged transition inside one mesh rather than a repaint or a swapped body.
- **structural.** SHEET: The bleached halo persists independently of the colony: in the damage panel the colony is reduced to a dark charred mat while the pale halo stays at full width around it. The stain is a record of where the colony has BEEN — that is what makes 'bleached flooring is the warning' a warning rather than a health bar.

## capbage — diverges / rebuild

The Capbage's leaves are built as 29 flat alpha cards instead of curved leaf-shell meshes, so the plant has no shell and no cavity — the sheet's hollow shelter is rendered as a solid black post standing in the middle of a flat rosette.

- **structural.** SHEET: Every leaf is a thick, volumetric, hood-shaped SHELL. The turnaround's side view (panel 2) shows fat egg-lobes with real depth and a visible thickness edge along each split seam; the hero shows the concave INNER face of the near leaves opposite the convex outer face of the far ones. The written brief is explicit: '10-14 large rigged curved leaf-shell meshes' and 'Avoid foliage cards'; the turnaround table row reads 'Same 12 rigged leaf-shell meshes'. The turnaround doc names Seefern and Hushbloom ONLY as the species whose side view must collapse to a razor-thin card — Capbage is pointedly not on that list.
  BUILD: build_capbage_rigged() emits 29 calls to b.card(..., art=CB_ART, segments=3). Builder.card is a single flat quad, two verts per row, zero thickness and zero curvature (builder.py:253-311). The side render collapses to exactly the razor-flat blades the doc reserves for Seefern/Hushbloom. This is the leaf-shell species built as the foliage-card species.
  FIX: Replace b.card with a curved shell primitive. Add a leaf_shell loft to paintlib/builder.py: a quad strip of N rows x 3-4 columns swept along a curved spine with a lateral concave arc and an offset back sheet giving it thickness, tagged with only a few large facets per the brief. Keep the row-major vertex emission order so a rig.shell_rows(start, rows, cols) companion to rig.card_rows can feed rig.weight_chain_strip unchanged — bones stay rows-1, so the density law holds by construction. The leaves are what HOLDS the form here, so this is the house law's mesh case, not its card case.
- **structural.** SHEET: The cavity is NEGATIVE SPACE. In the hero and in turnaround views 1 and 3 you look past the leaf rim into an empty recess whose walls are the PALE concave inner faces of the leaves, with a recessed floor below the rim. The black thumbnail proves it: the mouth is a NOTCH cut clean out of the filled silhouette — a hole, not a dark object. The brief: 'one large dark cavity with pale inner walls'.
  BUILD: b.ngon_prism((0,0), 0.22, 0.26, 0.40, 'cb_void', sides=10, z0=0.18) — a solid 10-sided opaque prism standing on top of the stem, painted channels.ground x0.12 = RGB(2,2,3), effectively pure black. This is the Meeb crater-vs-cone error in mirror image: a HOLE built as a POST. It plugs the space the player is supposed to read as a doorway, it can never produce the thumbnail's notch, and because both the leaves and the void are near-black there is no pale inner wall anywhere on the asset. The code comment concedes the risk ('or it reads as a column standing above the plant') and then ships the column anyway.
  FIX: Delete the cb_void prism outright. Build the cavity as real negative space: a shallow sunk floor (b.disc or a short inverted-taper ngon_prism) at roughly z 0.18, ringed by the concave inner faces of the new shell leaves, with those inner faces painted a PALE value so the recess reads as lit walls falling into shadow. The darkness must come from occlusion between shells, never from a black solid.
- **structural.** SHEET: Deep in the cavity, seated on a short fluted pedestal, sits a small pale bone-white spiky rosette — the meristem. It appears in the hero, in the splayed panel, and in both open turnaround views; it is the tended-ness read the brief calls 'a small meristem deep inside'.
  BUILD: Nothing at the cavity floor. The only central element is cb_flash: two crossed emissive cards at z=1.02, i.e. at and above the plant's apex (inner tier tips reach ~1.11), used solely as the tend-completion bright flash. The meristem is absent, and the flash fires from ABOVE the head rather than glowing out of the shelter.
  FIX: Model the pedestal and rosette as a small mesh at the cavity floor (~z 0.22-0.30): a fluted ngon_prism plus a low spiked crown of a few large facets, in the pale species colour. Give it its own bone if it should swell during capbage_tend. Move the cb_flash cards down to the cavity mouth (~z 0.55) so the tend completion reads as light coming OUT of the shelter, which is also what makes the flash legible when the plant is sealed.
- **structural.** SHEET: The sealed state (affordance panel 3, turnaround view 4) is a TENSE NEAR-SPHERE: a closed ball with broad overlap seams, each seam a pale rib, the surface continuous and volumetric. The brief: 'the same leaves fold into a tense near-sphere with broad overlap seams'.
  BUILD: capbage_seal curls 29 zero-thickness planes inward via rig.chain_wave. Flat planes converging on an axis cannot enclose a volume — they produce a spiky bud with gaps between blades, not a sealed shell. The clip's authoring (timing, the tended-vs-wild split, the premature slip) is correct and worth keeping; its ENDPOINT is unreachable with the current geometry.
  FIX: Falls out of the shell-mesh rebuild: once each leaf is a curved shell wide enough to wrap ~1/4 of the head, the same chain_wave amounts close it into a continuous sphere. Retune the per-tier amounts (currently 1.34/1.02/0.62) against the new four-layer tilts and verify the closed pose has no daylight between adjacent shells before re-authoring the seam glow.
- **silhouette.** SHEET: The black thumbnail is a compact, dense HOOD: a solid mass roughly 1.6 wide to 1 tall with leaf tips rising ABOVE the mouth and arching over it, and a clean notch bitten out of the top centre. It reads at distance as a shelter you could crawl into.

## crust — diverges / rebuild

The Meeb error, verbatim: the sheet's pores are craters bored INTO a pale ragged wax mat, and the build has them as flared cones standing 4.4 cm proud of a dark rectangular slab.

- **structural.** SHEET: Every pore is a crater sunk into the mat — a dark well whose inner walls you can see, lit on the upper lip and shadowed below. The brief names them "hexagonal crater-pores". Nothing protrudes from the mat's face in any of the four panels, including the dilated one, where the cells glow from INSIDE the wells.
  BUILD: The five working pores are `b.limb((px, -0.05, pz), (px, -0.152, pz), 0.062, 0.088, ...)` — tubes that run OUTWARD (colony grows toward -Y) and FLARE from r=0.062 to r=0.088 at the far end. The drawn array plane sits at ARRAY_Y = -0.108, so in the OPEN pose each vent stands 0.044 m proud of the mat surface and is widest at its outermost ring. Five outward funnels, not five bored wells.
  FIX: Invert the tube along Y in `build_crust`: run each mouth from the array plane INWARD toward the wall — `b.limb((px, -0.106, pz), (px, -0.03, pz), 0.088, 0.052, ...)` — so its widest ring is flush with the mat surface and it narrows going in. Keep `cap_start=False`; add a dark cap or backing face at the inner end so the well reads as a hole with a bottom rather than a see-through puncture. Drive dilation by scaling the mouth's OUTER radius (the iris widening at the surface), not by extruding the tube forward.
- **structural.** SHEET: An irregular thick waxy mat fused into the masonry, with a ragged, lobed, crack-edged boundary — one lobe peels down and to the right in the hero. The brief's explicit avoid-list opens with "a rectangular machine panel".
  BUILD: `b.box((0, PLATE_Y, 0), (SPAN_W=1.34, PLATE_D=0.055, SPAN_H=1.02))` plus a second `b.box` lobe at 0.60 × 0.62 of span — two nested rectangles with square corners. The colony's ragged outline exists ONLY in the alpha of `_pore_array_art` (`if r > 0.80 + 0.22*noise: continue`), and that card is mounted at y=-0.108, i.e. IN FRONT of both opaque boxes. The rectangle is therefore fully visible around the ragged card and is what the player sees — confirmed in both render cards.
  FIX: Make the boundary one shared function and give it to the GEOMETRY, not just the card alpha. Extract the ragged radius test from `_pore_array_art` into a `_margin_r(theta)` helper and build the slab as an n-gon prism through that boundary (paintlib's ngon path) instead of `b.box`. The alpha card must never be smaller or other-shaped than the solid it dresses — that is what turns the mat into a panel with a sticker on it.
- **structural.** SHEET: The dilate panel lights ONE CONTIGUOUS cluster of roughly a dozen adjacent cells on the left-of-centre of the mat, and the vapour curtain covers only that sub-span; the rest of the mat stays dark and passable. The brief: "one contiguous pore band dilates"; the batch-02 table: "local pore-band vent".
  BUILD: `MOUTH_CELLS = [(0,0), (-2,1), (1,1), (-2,-1), (1,-1)]` — a scattered quincunx spanning columns -2..+1 and rows -1..+1, i.e. opposite corners of the lattice. `_pose()` opens all five together, and the hazard card is `b.card((0, BAND_Y, 0), (SPAN_W * 1.04, BAND_H), ...)` — the full plate width plus 4%. There is no un-vented span of the plate, so the "visible bad band" cannot be routed around; the whole colony is the hazard.
  FIX: Make the working set contiguous and local: `MOUTH_CELLS = [(-1,0), (0,0), (1,0), (-1,1), (0,1)]` (adjacent cells, ~1/3 of the span), and size the hazard card to that cluster's extent with a matching x-offset — `b.card((cluster_cx, BAND_Y, 0), (SPAN_W * 0.42, BAND_H), ...)` — instead of `SPAN_W * 1.04`. The band's job is to take a lane, not the wall.
- **structural.** SHEET: The damaged panel is not an absent colony: a burned REGION has flaked away to bare stained wall while TWO remnant honeycomb clusters survive (one left-of-centre, one at the right). The brief: "a burned region flakes away to bare wall".

## unwelded limb chains — measured sweep (2026-08-11)

Every fauna build was scanned for UNWELDED SEAMS: open edges lying coincident

abutting rings that were never welded show up as pairs of open edges on top of

| hidra | 810 | 774 | **732** | 62 abutting prisms along a helix — FIXED 2026-08-11, now 444 verts / 42 open / 0 seams |

**The Hidra is the worst asset defect currently shipping.** Its body is one run
from tail cap to cutting tip, emitted as 62 separate prisms along a helix. A
helix changes axis at every joint, so every one of the 61 abutments is a real
gap — the creature reads as a stack of loose chunks strung on a wire, not a
coil. Worth knowing: the coil went through four revision passes that never
landed. They were adjusting proportions on a body that was never one surface.

### What a fix must respect (found by an adversarial review of two auto-drafted plans; both were REFUTED)

1. **The weight boundary may not land on the recess span.** With exclusive
   weights the crease falls on exactly one span per link, and the naive "a bone
   claims the ring at its TIP" rule puts it on the 12.5 mm pinch
   (`0.16 * SEG_LEN`), which cannot carry the shipped ~32.7 deg per-link bend —
   all 19 joints fold back through themselves. The boundary belongs at the
   link's own joint (`_spine(i)`), whose span is `0.45 * SEG_LEN` ~ 35 mm.
   The same tip-claiming rule IS correct on the Toxo, because its stations are
   evenly spaced and there is no short span to land on. The rule is not
   portable; the span lengths decide it.
2. **The body carries TWO parts.** `hd_recess` (the dark joint band) alternates
   with `hd_body`, and `Builder.tube` takes one part — a naive conversion erases
   the dark joints. Either `tube` learns a per-span part (one welded ring set,
   one atlas group per contiguous part-run, which works because UVs are
   per-loop so a shared ring may hold a different corner key in each group), or
   the recess moves entirely into the drawn skin card, which already paints
   "a near-black recess at every joint".
3. **`SEG_START` is read by the conduit surgery**, not only by the weighting.
   One tube emits rings consecutively and that index scheme stops existing.
4. **Re-audit UVs after.** A rebuild invalidates them, and the packer's tube
   island is new.

### The Gnawer's twelve are a FALSE POSITIVE — the detector's, and mine

Located them: three haze stations, each building TWO crossed cards at one centre
(`axis='Z'` and `axis='Y'`, `build_gnawer.py:216-221`). Two quads sharing a
centre also share edge MIDPOINTS at ±s/2 on their common axis, which is the key
the detector clusters on. Three stations × two sides × two edges = twelve.
Crossed billboard cards are supposed to intersect; nothing is unwelded and the
legs are fine. The auto-drafted plan proposed converting four leg chains that do
not have the defect, and this file previously said the same. Measure where a
seam IS before restructuring a rig around the count.

**Corollary for the detector:** coincident edge MIDPOINTS are necessary but not
sufficient. A genuine unwelded ring shows a whole ring's worth of edges (5 or 6
for these bodies); two edges in a cluster means crossed cards. Read the cluster
SIZE, not just the total.

### Where the weld boundary goes (the Hidra, applied and verified)

Exclusive weights put the entire crease between two bones on ONE span, so which
span is the whole decision. A bone takes its own joint, pinch and swell — rings
3i..3i+2 — leaving the crease on the swell-to-next-joint span, the longest in the
link at 0.45 of its length. Claiming the ring at the bone's TIP instead drops it
on the 12 mm pinch, where a 33 degree bend folds the quad back through itself.
Verified by rendering mid-`hidra_unspool`: joints continuous, no inversion.

`Builder.tube` now takes ONE NAME PER SPAN as well as one name for the run, so
the Hidra keeps its alternating `hd_recess` / `hd_body` banding. The rings stay
shared; only the paint groups divide, one per contiguous run of a name. That is
safe because UVs are per-LOOP, so the ring where two materials meet holds a
different corner key in each group without either tearing.

### redactor — the rebuild brief (read both sheets before starting)

`redactors-cloaked-v2.png` and `redactors-revealed-v2.png`. The two states are
nearly two bodies, and the encounter IS the change between them, so the rig
carries the whole thing.

**Cloaked.** A tall curved SICKLE-SHARD standing against a wall — roughly two to
three metres, wide and swept at the top into two horns, tapering to a point that
touches the floor. It wears the WALL's tiling, not its own skin, and a refraction
ripple runs down the seam where its two halves meet. It reads as architecture; a
player walks past it. Nothing in the build carries this form today.

**Revealed.** A long, low, segmented arthropod lying horizontal: a pale
crystalline carapace — the LIGHTEST mass in the frame, not the darkest — faceted
into plates, with roughly nine large amber orbs glowing THROUGH the shell along
its length, biggest at the head end and diminishing rearward, plus a lit orb at
the snout tip. Six-plus long thin jointed legs, each ending in a fine claw, spanning
well beyond the body. A distinct head end: the body tapers and has a facing.

**What the build has instead:** a radially symmetric near-black dome, stub legs
with no length or joints, no amber anywhere in any state, no head, no taper, no
segmentation, no sickle.

**How to build it, given what now exists:**

- The body is `Builder.tube` with per-span parts — a segmented arthropod is
  exactly the shape that facility was added for, and it gets the plate banding
  for free while staying one welded surface.
- The orbs are DRAWN (they repeat, and they are glows seen through a shell), on
  the same card-with-emissive arrangement the Naturalizer's granule windows use.
  Give each its own bone if they need to light in sequence.
- The legs are tubes, hip-knee-foot, with the crease at the knee — and their
  claws must be GRAFTED, not appended at the foot ring's centre, which is the
  shortcut the Naturalizer still owes.
- The cloak/reveal is one rigged transition, not two meshes swapped. The shard is
  the creature folded: legs drawn in along the body, carapace closed, the whole
  thing stood on end. Reveal unfolds it. Park it CLOAKED — a Redactor that spawns
  standing has spent its stealth before the player arrives.
- Palette: the carapace is PALE (near-white crystalline with cool blue-grey and
  faint violet), amber `species/flure_core` and `channels/lamp` for the orbs. The
  current near-black is inverted from the sheet and it is the material, not the
  render light.

### gasafoetida — the sealed pod still shows its cavity (diagnosed, not fixed)

The idle plant wears a black bored-out hole down every pod, which is the read its
sheet keeps for AFTER the burn. Diagnosed by recolouring `ga_char` to magenta and
rebuilding: the dots turned magenta, so the black is the cavity floor seen
straight down an open mouth. Not a texture bug, not the resin, not texel
starvation — three hypotheses that were each tested and each wrong.

**Why no plug fixes it.** The pod is three stacked prisms with `cap_top=False`, so
the mouth is genuinely open at `GA_POD_TOP`, r=0.032. A stopper sitting IN that
mouth cannot close it from above: seen from any raised angle there is a ring of
open cavity around the stopper. Widening it and then making it overhang both
shrank the hole without closing it.

**The fix.** A sealed serotinous cone has NO opening at rest, so the mouth needs a
CAP — a `ga_pod` disc at `GA_POD_TOP` on its own bone (`seal%d_0`), parked at 1.0
and scaled to 0.001 by `gasafoetida_open` and `gasafoetida_combust`. That is the
same parked-scale grammar the Seefern's eye cards and the Naturalizer's granule
windows already use, and it keeps the build's own principle that "the crown bores
open" needs something to open ONTO. It is rig plumbing — geometry, a bone per pod,
weights, and two pose updates — rather than a value change, which is why it is
written down here instead of guessed at a fifth time.

### meeb — the free-hoop finding, measured to a number (2026-08-12)

The audit said the cup lips still stand off as free hoops with background visible
through the arches. Chasing it produced two useless metrics and then one that
works, which is the part worth keeping.

**The metric that works.** A free hoop puts SKY THROUGH the silhouette, so: per
image column, count background pixels that have body BOTH above and below them.
Background around the object never qualifies; only a hole does.

    before  1582 px enclosed
    after    917 px enclosed   (-42%)

**What the fix did and did not do.** Each lip vertex now sits at the body's own
surface along ITS OWN direction rather than on a flat plane cut across a curved
body. Measured on the lip range in isolation — recorded deliberately, because the
nucleus and vacuoles stand proud BY DESIGN and swamped the earlier number —
**0 of 384 lip verts stand proud, worst is 1 mm INSIDE**. So the lips are flush
and the remaining 917 px is NOT them.

**What is still unknown.** Something else on this body encloses sky. Candidates
not yet eliminated: the foot lobes at the skirt, an organelle breaking the limb,
or a cup bored near enough the silhouette to open through it. Do not assume it is
the lip again — that has been measured out.

**Two metrics that did NOT work, recorded so nobody rebuilds them:**
- Whole-mesh standoff from the surface. Cannot isolate the defect: the nucleus
  and vacuoles are proud by design and dominate the count (93 verts >4 mm, worst
  130 mm, almost all legitimate).
- A fixed frame-fraction crop to sample "the halo" or "the arch". The render
  camera frames to the object's bounds, so changing the object's size zooms the
  image and the same crop stops covering the same region — it reported a WIDENED
  Candid halo as narrower.

**Localised (2026-08-12).** Flood-filling the enclosed sky into blobs puts
**630 of the 917 px in ONE blob at the top cup** (x213-276, y111-126), with 175
and 64 px at the right-flank rims and two specks elsewhere.

Combined with the lip measuring flush (0 of 384 verts proud) and the cutter
leaving a blind floor at `surf - CUP_DEPTH`, that rules out both a standoff and a
see-through. What is left is geometry that is CORRECT and still reads wrong: the
rim of a rear-facing cup lies on the far surface and projects above the crown from
a raised camera, so the line of sight between rim and crown passes over the body
and lands on background. A rim on the far side of a lumpy body, seen over the top.

So the remaining question is not "is the lip proud" — that is measured out — but
whether the sheet's cup PLACEMENT puts a mouth up there at all. Its silhouette
panel is a solid lobed blob with no ring anywhere in the outline, which suggests
the arrangement, not the rim, is what differs. Check `_cup_dir` against the
turnaround before touching lip geometry again.
