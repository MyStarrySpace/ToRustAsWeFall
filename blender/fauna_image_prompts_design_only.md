# Fauna Image Generation Prompts

A list of image generation prompts for each creature species in the game, intended for feeding into a generator to produce concept and reference imagery. Each prompt is self-contained: it can be pasted directly into a generator without additional context. Prompts assume the game's voxel-painterly aesthetic (Blockbench and Crocotile base geometry with painterly atmospheric textures).

Common style preamble that should be prepended or appended to every prompt:

> Voxel and low-poly base geometry with painterly atmospheric textures applied over it. Hand-painted brush detail visible on every surface. Restricted palette: muted teals and greens with rust-red and warm cream highlights, near-black background. Diorama-on-dark composition. Soft lighting from one direction, diffuse fill. Single specimen at frame center, isolated against the void. Subject reads as biological organism, not as machine.

## Entry structure

Each entry describes the species' visual design only:

- **Silhouette priority.** The one feature that must read at any distance and any zoom. If everything else dissolves, this is what identifies the species.
- **Form.** The static look: shape, proportions, color, material, surface detail.
- **States.** (Where applicable.) The visual appearance of each distinct state.
- **Hurt / death.** What flinches, breaks, or collapses when hit; what destroyed parts look like.

## Sapscraps

**Silhouette priority:** Three radial palps in three-fold radial symmetry around a central recessed mouth. From above, the organism is a triangular three-pointed shape; from the side, it is a low disc with three forward-projecting hooks. The three-fold radial body plan is the read.

**Form:** Disc-like body sitting low to the ground, roughly chest-high to a small dog. Three forward-projecting hooked palps emerge in three-fold radial symmetry from around a recessed central mouth-pit. Surface is a deep red-violet plated shell, simple and matte rather than detailed. The chelating clamps at the palp tips are the only sharply-rendered feature on the body; everything else stays simplified for visual economy.

**Hurt / death:** When hit, the body flattens slightly and one palp may snap off. On death, the disc collapses inward toward its center and the palps splay outward in a flat triradiate shape — the corpse reads as a small dark three-pointed star on the floor.

## Aembers

**Silhouette priority:** Translucent body with a single bright internal core. From any angle, the read is "soft glowing organism with a chartreuse-yellow heart visible through its skin."

**Form:** Soft shrimp-like body, gelatinous and slightly elongated, roughly the size of a small backpack. Outer surface pale green-yellow translucency, flexible rather than hard-shelled. Inside the body, a single visible glowing core glows chartreuse-yellow, the surrounding gel diffusing the glow outward into a halo. The core pulses slowly in a steady internal rhythm.

**Hurt / death:** When hit, the gel body deforms visibly and the core flickers. On death, the gel deflates and the core dims to a faint residual ember before going out entirely. The corpse is a flat translucent puddle with a small dark spot at the center where the core was, leaking a faint glow at the puddle's edges for a few seconds before fading.

## Hidras

**Silhouette priority:** Three-fold blade segments along a coiled body. Motionless, the silhouette flattens against a surface; alerted, the blades rotate slightly and the helix uncoils.

**Form:** Long multi-segmented body, roughly arm-thick. Each segment carries three radial blade-fins in three-fold radial symmetry. The body coils in a propeller-twist along its length, segments rotating gradually relative to each other. Blade fins are flat and broad, mottled grey-bronze with metallic sheen at the edges. Recesses between segments are darker, almost black. Eyes are small dark pinholes distributed along the body, one or two per segment.

**Hurt / death:** When hit, individual blade-fins can be sheared off — the damaged segment loses its three-fold symmetry. Severed segments separate from the body but continue twitching for a few seconds. On full death, the body uncoils flat against the substrate, blades drooping outward; the corpse reads as a long ribbed strip with the propeller-twist relaxed.

## Crusts

**Silhouette priority:** Hexagonal pore-array waxy mat fused into a wall section. The hexagonal close-packing is the read.

**Form:** A thick layered wax-mat colony covering a wall section, the surface architecture an intricate hexagonal close-packed pore array. Matte cream-pale between the pores, each pore a deep dark recess set in the hexagonal grid. Cross-section visible at the colony's edge: three or four layers of pore-arrays stacked vertically, the entire structure built into and partially fusing with the wall it grew on. Color shifts subtly across the colony from pale cream at the older center to faint rust-tint at the actively-growing edge. The boundary between Crust and substrate is effectively gone at the center.

**Hurt / death:** Damaged areas of the mat crack and flake away, exposing the bare wall beneath. Sustained burning clears the colony, leaving scorched bare wall.

## Candids

**Silhouette priority:** Three-strata layered mold-mat architecture. The basal carpet, the chained mid-layer, and the upper canopy must read as three distinct horizontal bands.

**Form:** A mold-like mat colony with three architectural strata visible in cross-section. Bottom: dense pebbled carpet of small round cells. Middle: forest of vertical chained cells stretching upward in connected segments. Top: horizontal canopy of branched filaments forming a thread-like cap. Pale yellow-cream throughout, with the basal layer slightly darker (older basal cells) and the canopy slightly translucent (newer filaments). Around the colony, a bleached corrosive zone where the surrounding floor is visibly discolored and pitted.

**Hurt / death:** Burning chars the canopy layer, exposing the chained mid-layer beneath; sustained burning collapses the colony down through its strata until only the basal cell layer remains. Charred patches show in the canopy, the layered structure exposed where it has burned away.

## Meebs

**Silhouette priority:** Translucent blob with multiple food cups (mouth-pits) distributed across the body surface, oriented in all directions. The "many mouths" reading is the species ID.

**Form:** A translucent shapeless blob-like organism roughly the size of a small dog, gelatinous and roughly spherical at rest, deforming continuously as it moves. Multiple food cups (deep concave pits in the body surface) scattered across the body, each puckering outward like a mouth-pit. The body is translucent enough to show internal organization: a central dark core, small pulsing bubbles, and darker sacs full of partially-digested material visible as irregular shapes. Color is pale green-grey, almost transparent at the edges.

**Hurt / death:** When hit, the gel body deforms and lobes retract briefly. Sustained damage causes the body to lose cohesion: it visibly thins at the edges, the internal structures exposed through increasingly transparent body. On death, the gel collapses into a flat puddle, the core and sacs settling at the bottom. The puddle remains briefly visible before being absorbed back into the substrate.

## Naturalizers

**Silhouette priority:** Low quadrupedal-or-hexapodal beetle-like body with a translucent dorsal carapace, glowing internal granule clusters visible through the carapace as warm orange-yellow dots packed together like pomegranate seeds. The "internal lights through translucent shell" is the read at any distance.

**Form:** A polished beetle-like organism walking on six short stubby limbs, body close to the ground, deceptively soft posture. Dorsal carapace is grey-blue with translucent patches across the back revealing dense granule clusters inside, glinting warm orange-yellow. The granules are packed tightly inside the carapace, visible as a stippled luminous interior. A single sharper pale yellow band across the head where sensory organs cluster. The silhouette is decidedly NOT humanoid: low, focused, polished.

**Hurt / death:** When hit, the dorsal carapace can crack, exposing more of the internal granule field. Heavy damage causes granules to leak out as small glowing droplets that fall to the substrate. On death, the legs collapse and the body settles flat, the carapace cracking fully and the granule field dimming over a few seconds before going dark. The corpse leaves a faint warm afterglow on the floor briefly before fading.

## Redactors

**Silhouette priority:**
- *Cloaked:* a section of background that has peeled forward into space, locally perfect mimicry but the form is detectable as a vertical plane of surface-detail standing where no surface should be.
- *Revealed:* an elongated, malformed Naturalizer-derived body, longer-than-tall with irregular granule clusters and too many limbs.

**Cloaked-state form:** A corridor or interior space in which one section of the wallpaper, floor tile, framed poster, or architectural detail in the background appears to have peeled forward into the space — a vertically-oriented plane of surface-detail standing in three-dimensional space. The cloaking is locally perfect: the surface is exactly what is behind it, copied with high fidelity, but the surface tension between what the cloak displays and what is actually behind betrays the form. The viewer notices the discrepancy as a wrongness: a doorway slightly displaced, a fern frond hovering an inch ahead of where its plant pot is, a tile pattern continuing where it shouldn't. The body underneath is invisible.

**Revealed-state form:** A body that reads as Naturalizer-derived but stretched and malformed. Longer than tall, with the polished-beetle silhouette of the Naturalizer elongated into something that walks more uprightly. Translucent carapace patches still show internal granule clusters but the granules are malformed, smaller, and unevenly distributed; the elegant pomegranate-seed packing of the Naturalizer is here irregular, with empty patches and overdense clusters. The carapace surface itself has a faint residual ghost of whatever it was just cloaked as, like an afterimage that has not fully cleared. Eight limbs articulated more loosely than the Naturalizer's six. Color is paler than the Naturalizer's grey-blue, washed out, with the warm granule-glow dimmer and uneven.

**Hurt / death:** When damaged, the cloak drops and the body is revealed. Revealed, the carapace cracks like a Naturalizer's but the cracks reveal more empty space inside than granules. On death, the body collapses laterally and the residual cloak-ghost flickers visibly across the surface a few times before settling, the corpse freezing whatever surface it last copied.

## Gnawers

**Silhouette priority:** Low-slung quadruped with a wide, drooping flesh-dissolving maw and a visible digestive haze around the head. The "drooling pursuit-hunter" silhouette is the read.

**Form:** A long ratlike quadruped with sleek oily-black coat carrying a faint red-purple blood-dark sheen, hunched predatory posture, body roughly the size of a medium dog but lower and longer. The mouth is wide and drooping, with a constant pale digestive mist hanging in the air around the jaws and trailing behind the head. Eyes small, set far forward in a low skull. Dark blood-red pigmentation darkest around the mouth and along the lower jaw. Skin is oily and slightly wet-looking.

**Hurt / death:** When hit, the Gnawer recoils backward and the digestive cloud disperses briefly before re-forming. Heavy damage exposes the underlying tissue beneath the oily coat — pale tissue showing through tears in the skin, contrasting with the dark exterior. On death, the body collapses sideways in a long sprawl, the digestive cloud lingering around the corpse for a few seconds before dispersing.

## Spikers

**Silhouette priority:** Asymmetric single-spire geometry. Triangular base, ONE thick stalk reaching straight up to a single branched crown at the top, basal filaments splaying at the floor. The single upward-reaching stalk capped with a branched crown is the read.

**Form:** Anchored to the substrate by a triangular base-body, from which one thick stalk rises straight upward like a periscope, terminating in a single branched crown at the top branching outward like a fan of bare twigs. Basal filaments splay at the floor like splayed roots around the base. The trunk is pale teal-white with darker veining along its length tracing a path from base to top. The triangular base-body is the densest, most clearly defined part of the body. The crown branches at the top point in roughly all horizontal directions.

**Hurt / death:** When hit, the trunk's veining flickers and the crown dims briefly. Heavy damage shears off branches of the upper crown. With enough damage the trunk cracks and slumps; on death it falls and breaks at the base, the crown shattering on impact, the splayed basal filaments left at the floor like a dead root system. An alternate death has the trunk wilt from inside, the veining going dark in patches before the structure crumbles inward.

## Tanglers

**Silhouette priority:** Double-helix body with grappling filament-extensions. The two intertwined strands are the read; the limbs are extensions of the helix itself, not separate appendages.

**Form:** Two intertwined filament strands coiling around each other in a left-handed helix, the body a continuous corrupted twist of paired strands. Body posture is low and hunched, the central twist sometimes near-collapsed and sometimes elongated. From the surface of the helix, additional filament extensions emerge and unspool outward: grappling limbs that are themselves more helix-strands uncoiling outward, with hooked filament ends. No defined head; the front is wherever the body is currently advancing. Color is dark olive-brown with paler highlights along the helix's outer edges, fine ribbing visible along each strand giving a fibrous-filament texture.

**Hurt / death:** When hit, the helix loosens partially, the two strands separating slightly before re-twisting. Sustained damage causes the strands to fully separate at one end, shortening the body. Filament-extensions can be severed; severed filaments writhe briefly on the floor before going inert. On death, the helix unwinds entirely, the two strands lying flat against the substrate in a tangled pile that slowly dissolves into fibrous dust over a few seconds.

## Flares

**Silhouette priority:** Translucent rounded body with a multi-lobed bead-string core visible centrally, surrounded by three classes of granules in three colors. The "translucent ball with internal beads" is the read.

**Form:** A rounded translucent body roughly the size of a beach ball, sitting low to the ground or slightly above it. Two defining internal features visible through the body: a multi-lobed core resembling a string of three or four connected beads at the body's center, and three distinct classes of granules packed densely around the core — small dark purple primary, medium pale yellow secondary, larger pale green tertiary. The granules are scattered through the interior in roughly equal numbers but at three different sizes and colors, giving the interior a stippled multi-color reading. Body surface is pale yellow-cream with a subtle warm tone, neutral and unthreatening at rest.

**Hurt / death:** When hit, the body surface punctures and granule contents leak out as small colored droplets. Heavy damage triggers a premature burst. On death without bursting, the body deflates into a flat puddle, the bead-string core and remaining granules visible at the bottom. After a full burst, only the deflated body and scattered granule droplets remain, slowly fading into the substrate.

## Toxos

**Silhouette priority:** Crescent body with a visible spiral front-cone and secretion-bulbs at the leading point. The "moon-shape with a small spiral cone at one tip" is the read.

**Form:** A small crescent-bodied organism, roughly the size of a small cat. Body curved like a banana, one side longer than the other, the curvature concave toward what reads as the "front." At the leading point of the crescent, a small visible front-cone: a pale spiral cone that extends and retracts, flanked by two slightly bulbous secretion-bulbs visibly holding secretion sacs. The rear half of the crescent body holds a darker round mass — a dark core visible through the body wall. Color is muted institutional grey with a faint reddish tinge throughout, but the apex structures (cone and bulbs) read in a paler cream that stands out against the body. Small relative to other organisms.

**Hurt / death:** When hit, the crescent body curls inward defensively, the cone retracting back into the body. Heavy damage causes the body to lose its crescent shape, deflating into a more rounded sad form. On death, the body collapses and the apex structures release their secretion sacs harmlessly into the substrate, pale cream droplets fading on the floor.
